**CPU usage** refers to the amount of work a computer's Central Processing Unit (CPU) is doing at any given moment. 

Think of the CPU as the "brain" of your computer. Just as your brain uses more energy and focus when you are solving a complex math problem than when you are daydreaming, your CPU uses more "effort" when running heavy programs than when sitting idle.

Here is a breakdown of what CPU usage actually means in practical terms:

### 1. How it is measured (Percentage)
CPU usage is almost always expressed as a **percentage (%)**.
* **0% Usage:** The CPU is "idle." It is powered on but not performing any active tasks.
* **1% – 30% Usage:** Normal for light tasks like browsing the web, typing a document, or listening to music.
* **50% – 80% Usage:** The CPU is working hard. This happens during video editing, high-end gaming, or running complex calculations.
* **100% Usage:** The CPU is "maxed out." It is working as fast as it possibly can. If it stays at 100% for a long time, your computer will likely feel slow, laggy, or "frozen" because there is no leftover capacity to handle new instructions.

### 2. What causes high CPU usage?
Several things can drive that percentage up:
* **Demanding Software:** Video games, video rendering software (like Adobe Premiere), and 3D modeling tools require massive amounts of calculation.
* **Background Processes:** Even if you aren't "doing" anything, your computer is running background tasks like antivirus scans, system updates, or cloud syncing (like OneDrive or Dropbox).
* **Too Many Browser Tabs:** Modern web browsers (like Chrome) can be very heavy on the CPU, especially if you have dozens of tabs open with video or heavy animations.
* **Malware/Viruses:** Some malicious programs (like crypto-miners) run in the background to use your computer's power for their own purposes, causing high usage.
* **Operating System Tasks:** Sometimes Windows or macOS needs to perform "housekeeping" tasks, which can cause temporary spikes.

### 3. How to check your CPU usage
If your computer feels slow, you can see exactly what is using your CPU:
* **On Windows:** Press `Ctrl + Shift + Esc` to open the **Task Manager**. Click the "CPU" column to sort by the programs using the most power.
* **On Mac:** Press `Command + Space` and type **Activity Monitor**. Look at the "% CPU" column.

### 4. Is high CPU usage bad?
* **It's fine if:** It is temporary. If you open a game and the usage jumps to 90%, that is normal. The CPU is doing exactly what it was designed to do.
* **It's bad if:** It stays at 100% constantly while you aren't doing anything. This leads to **overheating**, which can cause the computer to shut down to protect itself, and it will make your system feel extremely sluggish.
