1. **Empirical Approach** – Relies on observable, measurable evidence rather than pure speculation.
2. **Scientific Method** – Uses a structured process: forming hypotheses, designing experiments, collecting data, and drawing conclusions.
3. **Objectivity** – Strives to minimize bias and let evidence guide conclusions.
4. **Reproducibility** – Ensures findings can be verified and replicated by others.
5. **Peer Review** – Submits work to scrutiny by the scientific community.
6. **Curiosity &amp; Inquiry** – Driven by a desire to understand how and why things work.
7. **Communication** – Shares findings through publications, presentations, and collaboration.

## Important Nuances
- **No single credential** is required — a scientist can be a professional researcher, a student, or an amateur who follows scientific principles.
- **Discipline matters less than method** — a biologist, physicist, or sociologist are all "scientists" if they use rigorous, evidence-based inquiry.
- **The title vs. the practice** — One can *be* a scientist by *doing* science, not just by holding a job title.

In short, what defines a scientist is less about *what* they study and more about *how* they study it — through disciplined, evidence-based, and transparent inquiry.</pre>
